# Axie Chance — Product Vision

**Title:** Axie Chance
**Track:** Axie Infinity Vibeathon
**Platform:** Web (desktop and mobile, nothing to install)
**Session length:** 3–4 minutes per match

---

## 1. The pitch in one line

**Chain symbols for as long as you dare.** Every card you draw makes your hit bigger; a single card that doesn't connect wipes it out entirely. That's everything you need to understand to play, and everything it takes to be unable to stop.

---

## 2. Why it exists: a new category for the ecosystem

Competitive games on Ronin all ask for the same thing up front: learn a skill tree, memorize a card meta, understand an economy before you get to have fun. They are excellent games **for whoever is already inside**.

Axie Chance aims at the other end of the funnel. Its mechanic is so simple it explains itself in one sentence and clicks in a single turn, yet the decision it poses —*draw one more, or stand?*— is the same one that carries the most addictive board games ever made. That combination opens a category the ecosystem doesn't have yet:

- **Genuinely casual.** No twenty-minute tutorial, no glossary, no wallet to get started. Open a link and play.
- **Instant, visible tension.** The suspense needs no context: anyone watching the screen understands that the next card could be worth everything or nothing. It's the ideal format for streaming, short clips, and playing next to someone.
- **Pocket-sized sessions.** Three minutes. It fits in a supermarket queue and it fits in a tournament.
- **A front door into the Axie universe.** Players learn the six classes, their strengths and their anatomy **by playing**, not by reading a wiki. By the time they reach the deeper games on Ronin, they already speak the language.

This is the game an Axie player hands to a friend who has never touched Axie.

---

## 3. The heart of the game

### The decision

On your turn you draw from your personal deck. The first card opens one or more **chains** (one per symbol it carries). Every card after that has to share at least one symbol with a live chain: if it does, it extends it. If it shares none with any live chain, the **chain breaks** and your attack that round is worth 0.

At any moment you can **stand** and unload the damage you've piled up onto the rival Axie.

That's the whole game. Two buttons: **draw** or **stand**.

### Why it's fun

**Damage is quadratic, not linear.** A run of 2 is worth 4, a run of 3 is worth 9, a run of 4 is worth 16. Every extra card is worth far more than the last, so the game keeps pushing you forward while the risk climbs right alongside it. It isn't a cold math problem: it's a temptation.

**Greed has a visible shape.** The odds dial tells you, card by card, the real chance you have of staying alive. It turns from green to red right in front of you. Nobody busts "out of bad luck": they bust because they **chose** to go for one more with 40% staring back.

**Every turn is a story with an ending.** You walk away with a short, safe run, or you go for the chain of five and come back with 25 damage and shaking hands, or you bust on the last card and take nothing. A three-minute match is ten stories like that.

**Parallel runs add depth without adding complexity.** An opening card with three symbols opens three paths at once; the ones that don't connect freeze, keeping what they already earned. The new player sees "lots of chances to keep going". The expert sees a decision tree. Both of them are playing it right.

**Your deck changes while you play.** At the end of each turn you pick from the **central market** —six face-up cards, shared— and whatever you take is shuffled into your deck immediately. A successful attack lets you take one power card or two plain ones; a bust still leaves you one plain card, so a losing match is never a dead match. Nobody is locked out of the comeback.

**The powers are characters, not stats.** The poison vial that gets hurled, the egg that bounces in and leaves a shield that strikes back when it cracks, the snail that cuts the rival's next hit in half, the energy drink that spills over the damage number and makes it count upward. Twelve class powers, six live per match —one per class—, each with its own gesture, sound and moment. Players don't learn a table: they remember the octopus.

**The ending has one last breath.** Whoever drops to 0 HP gets a **Last Chance**: one more turn, wreathed in fire, to take the rival down with them. If they pull it off, the match ends in a draw. Plenty of matches are decided right there, and every one of them gets retold afterwards.

**The clock keeps the pulse.** 30 seconds to decide, 20 per market pick. There's never dead time, and never analysis paralysis.

## 4. Your Axie is your deck

The Axie Core integration isn't a skin and it isn't a bought advantage: it's **consistency math**.

Every Axie walks into the arena with a 10-card deck derived from its genetics:

- **6 favorable cards**, one per body part —Eyes, Ears, Horn, Mouth, Back, Tail—, each carrying its base class symbol plus a secondary symbol set by that part.
- **4 unfavorable cards** derived from the Axie's rock-paper-scissors triad: ally and prey appear twice each, the counters barely once. The deck is tilted in your favor by design, not by chance.

**Part Evolution as a variance reducer.** Evolving a part on-chain adds a **third symbol** to that part's card. It doesn't raise your damage directly: it multiplies your ways to connect. More connections mean fewer busts, longer chains and the freedom to play more aggressively. Evolution doesn't necessarily make you hit harder; it makes you **braver**.

**The Ronin flywheel:**
1. **Play** and earn AXP based on the length of your chains and the damage you land.
2. **Evolve** parts on-chain on App.Axie with that AXP.
3. **Optimize**: the evolved part comes back as a better card in your starting deck.
4. **Collect and breed**, hunting for anatomy combinations with real synergy in the central market.

## 5. The future: amulets you craft and spend

Mid-term progression has a single design goal: **give players things to craft without breaking the tension at the table**.

**Amulets that expire.** Powers will be craftable as limited-use amulets: they last a number of matches, rounds or activations, and then they're spent. You build them with what you earn by playing, equip them before the duel, and feel them for as long as they last.

Why they expire:

- **They keep the economy alive.** There's always something to craft, something to spend and a reason to come back tomorrow.
- **They prevent the final build.** Nobody reaches a permanent optimal setup that freezes the meta. Every season the arsenal renews itself, because the arsenal runs out.
- **They make equipping a real decision.** Spending the good amulet *today*, in this match, against this rival, carries weight. So does saving it.
- **They reward playing, not hoarding.** Whoever plays a lot has a full workshop; whoever bought once does not.

---

## 6. The promise: there's always a chance

This is the design pillar holding up everything above.

**Even fully maxed out, you can lose.** A player with every part evolved, the best deck and the most expensive amulets equipped is still one card away from busting. Upgrades move the probability; they never remove it. The ceiling of this game isn't certainty: it's *a very good chance*.

This matters for three reasons, and all three are commercial as much as they are design:

1. **The newcomer is never out of the fight.** Someone who opened the game ten minutes ago can beat the veteran, and will come back to try again. There's no wall at the entrance to discourage them.
2. **The veteran is never bored.** Their edge is real and it shows in every long run, but they still have to play well. Tension is not for sale.
3. **Winning always means something.** When victory isn't guaranteed, victory gets celebrated. That's the clip people share.

In Axie Chance luck isn't what happens to you: it's what you choose to push. And there is always, always a chance.

## 7. Zero friction, a clear road to Web3

- **Open it and play.** Native HTML5 and JavaScript, no install, no wallet, nothing to sign for the first match.
- **You learn by playing.** A tutorial of five playable rounds, with no modals and no walls of text: an arrow, a glow, and the match carrying on.
- **Play alone or together.** A level campaign against the Origins starters, duels against a CPU at the difficulty you choose, and online 1v1 matches in a room with a code and a QR —send a friend in any country a link and you're playing.
- **Conversion is a step, not a leap.** Connect Ronin Waypoint and the game reads the Axie's metadata and its Part Evolutions and builds the personalized deck on its own. The player doesn't switch games: the same game becomes theirs.

---

## 8. Where it's headed

**On-chain integration.** Ronin Waypoint and the Axie Core API to read real genes and Part Evolutions, procedural assignment of the six anatomy cards from the connected Axie, and real AXP accreditation against the official Sky Mavis endpoint.

**The amulet workshop.** Crafting expiring amulets and deck upgrades, with a materials economy fed by playing.

**Competition.** Real-time matchmaking, seasons with leaderboards and a King of the Hill mode.

**Collecting you can see.** Cosmetic support for Mystic, Origin and Shiny Axies with exclusive auras and attack effects: rare has to look rare on the table.

---

## 9. Who it's for

- **Casual and mobile players** who want short, intense matches that explain themselves.
- **Axie holders and collectors** who for the first time see their Part Evolutions translated into something they feel turn by turn.
- **Content creators and streaming audiences**: readable tension, visible luck, epic comebacks and thirty-second clips that need no setup.
- **Everyone who hasn't entered the ecosystem yet** and needs a first door that asks for nothing in return.
